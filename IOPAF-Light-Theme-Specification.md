# IOPAF Light Executive Assurance Theme

The public website will move from a dark navy-dominant composition to a light editorial system. The assessment engine remains unchanged. Authentic engine captures retain their original interface colors and are framed as evidence artifacts within the lighter public narrative.

| Role | Color | Intended use |
|---|---|---|
| Canvas | `#F4F1EA` | Warm ivory page background |
| Surface | `#FFFFFF` | Primary content and image frames |
| Surface muted | `#E8EDF1` | Secondary metadata and inactive tabs |
| Ink | `#10283A` | Primary typography and institutional framing |
| Ink muted | `#566B79` | Supporting explanation |
| Cobalt | `#225EA8` | Structural tabs, section identifiers and navigation |
| Teal | `#0B8178` | Evidence, assurance, connectors and primary actions |
| Amber | `#B26A12` | Draft, provisional and unevidenced states only |
| Red | `#B43D35` | Risk, exceptions, caps and residual exposure only |
| Rule | `#C7D2D9` | Borders and evidence-ledger dividers |

The hero uses ivory rather than a dark field, with a restrained cobalt assurance grid. The header becomes white with an ink wordmark and teal evidence rule. Workbenches retain dark ink navigation strips only where needed for tab clarity, while their surrounding chapters alternate ivory and white. The access section uses a pale cobalt wash rather than a dark panel. Full-screen image inspection retains a dark scrim for focus and readability.

## Premium refinement

The polished release reduces bright cobalt competition and replaces it with a quieter **mineral blue** (`#365F72`) for structure. Teal remains the only active assurance color. The page alternates warm parchment (`#F3F0E8`), near-white (`#FCFCFA`), cool mist (`#E9EFED`) and pale slate (`#E6ECEF`) so chapters transition without reverting to large dark bands.

Every chapter receives a short teal evidence rule, section introductions use a restrained teal ledger edge, and section numbers sit on white evidence tiles. Workbench tabs use mineral blue with teal active states; borders move to a warmer grey-green, and shadows become softer and less blue. Amber and red remain semantic and are not introduced as decorative accents.
