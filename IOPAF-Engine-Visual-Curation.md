## Final asset set

The website should use five authentic engine images: focused Controls workspace, Bayesian summary, Bayesian network, control interdependencies and Reports centre. The full 148-control dashboard remains available as source evidence, but its extreme page height makes it unsuitable for direct website placement; a dedicated viewport crop will be derived from it.

## Crop verification

The focused-assessment crop keeps the object selector, eight control domains, queue, control requirement, assurance path, maturity choice and posterior-failure panel readable at website scale. The Bayesian-network crop retains the complete left-to-right causal story from prior and group posteriors through likelihood/consequence to the residual-risk distribution, including the audited-model and provisional-calibration statements.

The dedicated dashboard crop preserves the 148-control estate totals, Critical/Core/Non-critical split, formal taxonomy counts, effective-maturity distribution, connected assurance path, filters and representative domain tiles in one legible frame. The Reports centre crop clearly shows visual PDF characteristics, whole-assessment exports, object assurance/Bayesian export and per-practice PDF/CSV delivery without exposing an excessively tall page.

## Three-stream correction

The authentic Standards page is exceptionally comprehensive but too tall for direct use at 13,016 pixels. Its strongest website regions are the top standards relationship architecture and the detailed CMMI-DEV/TMMi/ITIL/CMMI-SVC explanation and mapping views; these should be presented as separate curated crops rather than a thin whole-page thumbnail.

The Journey page is already a strong, self-contained website visual. It shows the nine lifecycle stages, maturity health, weakest and strongest stages, and the full SDLC / IT Operations / IAM swimlane. A crop centred on the stage cards and three-stream swimlane will directly answer how standards govern ideation-to-operation delivery.

The CMMI-DEV assessment screenshot shows Requirements Development with CMMI-DEV context, a seven-criterion spider, evidence-gated provisional maturity, a correction action and question-level source mapping. The IT Operations screenshot presents the same defensible method for Incident Management with explicit ITIL Version 5 and CMMI-SVC references. Their shared visual grammar proves that the engine applies one evidence model without erasing stream-specific standards.

The TMMi screen adds the missing dedicated testing-process story: Test Policy & Strategy carries TMMi and ISO/IEC/IEEE 29119 context, its own spider profile, evidence gate and correction-action requirement. The process-spider Results view demonstrates cross-practice visual comparison, but its first viewport is dominated by IT Operations because of catalogue order; it should support the analytics narrative rather than serve as the sole cross-stream proof.

The process heatmap clearly exposes the seven dimensions, score, effective level and evidence-capped weakness pattern across IT Operations and later SDLC rows. The Gaps & Roadmap screen converts low-scoring process questions into an actionable owner/date/status register with visible ITIL Version 5 and CMMI-SVC traceability. Together they provide the non-Control analytical and improvement evidence missing from the previous website release.

The curated Standards crop retains the top source-framework explanation, the full ideation-to-operation standards map and the three-stream architecture showing CMMI-DEV/TMMi, ITIL Version 5/CMMI-SVC and the IAM control library as parallel inputs to one engine. The curated Journey crop is highly legible and preserves both stage health and the complete Development / IT Operations / IAM swimlane, making it the primary visual answer to the user’s correction.

Both views use the engine’s authentic navy, teal, red, mono metadata and technical evidence-ledger hierarchy. They are stronger than synthetic website diagrams because they demonstrate actual working screens and dense decision context.
